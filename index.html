The letters of my name, mapped to musical pitches. Each letter is matched to the first note name that begins with it, reading across the naming systems of English, German, Dutch, solfège, Byzantine, Japanese and Indian music, shown with the matched letter in capitals. Where a letter hides inside a note name, I use that too: S is always E♭ (eS), I can be mI or tI, O is sOh and U is Ut. X has no match, so it's a wild card: I chose F♯. This mapping gives pitch material only. Rhythm, harmony and form come later, when I mine the material through the processes behind a piece. Try it with your name.

About

Roadmap and intuition

I'm a composer interested in the music that is already present in things: in names, words, places and texts. My work explores the balance between having a roadmap for a piece and the freedom to compose intuitively within it, a balance each new starting point invites me to strike differently.

My music has been performed by the BBC Symphony Orchestra, BBC National Orchestra of Wales, Onyx Brass, London Contemporary Music Group and Gemini. It has been broadcast on BBC Radio 3 and heard at the Cheltenham Festival, York Spring Festival, Waterford New Music Week, the National Eisteddfod, St David's Hall, Colston Hall and Wales Millennium Centre.

Alongside my own work, I've arranged, orchestrated and edited for Faber Music, Boosey & Hawkes, Schott, John Wilson, Huw Watkins and Helen Grime. I'm also active as a pianist, cellist, singer and conductor.

Research through composition

Three threads run through my work

i

Mined material

Many of my pieces grow from something outside music, such as a name, a poem or the story of a place. I translate it into notes and let its own properties suggest harmonies, melodies and forms. A single source can go on giving: material from one piece often becomes the seed of the next.

  • Tears, Idle Tears2026
  • Vignettes2026
  • Y Gors Fawr2018
  • The Way of Things2016
  • Tiny Symphony2016
ii

Compression

I'm drawn to miniature forms, such as a full symphony in a few minutes or an opera of only six words. They ask how much a listener can experience in very little time, and whether brevity can make concert music more open to new audiences.

  • Tiny Symphony2016
  • Poor Form2011
  • A glance at the painting in the hallway2011
  • 3 Years2009
  • A bit of a nightmare scenario2009
iii

The augmented performer

Working with coders and visual artists, I explore how technology can extend what a live musician can do. That might be a pianist duetting with an improvising computer, or an improvisation that is turned into projected visuals in real time, so the performer tells a visual story as well as a musical one.

  • Concerto for Augmented Piano2025
  • Piano Mapping2022–
  • Manhattan Circus2017
  • Tenebrae2016

Teaching and leadership

At UWE Bristol

I'm Associate Director for Audio and Music Technologies, overseeing our degrees in BSc (Hons) Audio and Music Technology and BSc (Hons) Creative Music Technology and the team who teach them. I recently led our team in a complete redevelopment of both programmes, and I'm external examiner for Music Technology at the University of Portsmouth.

I teach composition, music theory and analysis, and I love helping students find their own voice through them. I also enjoy supervising research projects, and I welcome enquiries from prospective PhD students in composition, particularly in the areas described above.
